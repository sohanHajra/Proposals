## 2026-11-18 (Wed) · passive orders with a virtual queue marker, dev day
- **Built** `src/s2f/passive.py` (`make passive`, five more tests, fifteen pass). A 0.01 BTC order joins the best price on its own side at decision time plus delay. The queue ahead is the displayed size at arrival. I follow my level with the top of book updates, and with the five level snapshot while a better price sits in front of me. Trades come first, then book updates. Three cancellation rules run on the same path and trades (front, random with seed 20261115, back). A cancel is any drop in displayed size that trades do not explain, and a rise joins behind me. If my price leaves the top five I count the order as unfilled from then on. 8,425 decisions<!--c:taker.n-->, nine delays, same decisions as the taker leg.
- **Result (`results/passive/passive_dev_2026-09-01.json`).** Fill probability within 10 s barely moves with delay and the rule moves it a lot. At 1 ms it is 68.3%<!--c:passive.front.fill1--> (front), 54.9%<!--c:passive.random.fill1--> (random) and 46.7%<!--c:passive.back.fill1--> (back). At 500 ms it is 68.3%<!--c:passive.front.fill500-->, 55.1%<!--c:passive.random.fill500--> and 46.7%<!--c:passive.back.fill500-->. Slope of fill probability on log delay per rule, with 95% block bootstrap intervals. Front 0.00018<!--c:passive.front.slope--> (negative 0.00068<!--c:passive.front.slope.lo--> to 0.00103<!--c:passive.front.slope.hi-->), random 0.00027<!--c:passive.random.slope--> (negative 0.00049<!--c:passive.random.slope.lo--> to 0.00108<!--c:passive.random.slope.hi-->), back negative 0.00009<!--c:passive.back.slope--> (negative 0.00075<!--c:passive.back.slope.lo--> to 0.00059<!--c:passive.back.slope.hi-->). All three cover zero. The sign is not consistently negative, so H2 would not count as a finding on this day.
- **H2b (my hypothesis).** Drop in fill probability from 1 ms to 500 ms, back minus front, is 0.0005<!--c:passive.h2b--> with interval negative 0.0050<!--c:passive.h2b.lo--> to 0.0059<!--c:passive.h2b.hi-->. No sign of an interaction on this day.
- **Surprise.** The gap between rules (about 22 points, 68.3%<!--c:passive.front.fill1--> under front against 46.7%<!--c:passive.back.fill1--> under back) is huge next to anything delay does. That fits Danait et al. qualitatively, though their gaps were measured on Tokyo equities and are not comparable in size.
- **Markout, secondary (H2c).** The 10 s maker markout of filled orders is negative under all three rules, about negative 0.4<!--c:passive.front.m10_1--> bps (front), negative 0.7<!--c:passive.random.m10_1--> (random), negative 1.0<!--c:passive.back.m10_1--> (back) at 1 ms. At 500 ms the unconditional figures are within 0.03 bps of those.
- **Matched markouts run against H2c.** On decisions that fill at both 1 ms and 500 ms (5,388<!--c:passive.front.nboth--> under front, 4,283<!--c:passive.random.nboth--> under random, 3,708<!--c:passive.back.nboth--> under back), the 10 s markout is better at 500 ms than at 1 ms by 0.067<!--nc--> bps (front, negative 0.512<!--c:passive.front.m10both_1--> against negative 0.446<!--c:passive.front.m10both_500-->), 0.062<!--c:passive.random.m10diff--> bps (random) and 0.088<!--c:passive.back.m10diff--> bps (back). The 1 s markouts move the same way, by about 0.034<!--c:passive.front.m1diff--> bps under each rule. *(Corrected 2026-11-19, see the entry on tying numbers to results files.)* H2c predicts the opposite sign. I computed no bootstrap interval for these differences, so they are point estimates on one day. An interval goes into `passive.py` before the test days.
- **Across rules, exploratory.** Back rule fills lose more than front rule fills. That is consistent in direction with Yueshen's end of queue prediction, but it compares cancellation rules, not measured queue positions. Back rule fills are also a selected set, since they only happen after more volume trades at my price, so part of the gap is selection. It is not H2c, which is about delay.
- **Many orders lose their level.** At every delay, in 37.6%<!--c:passive.lefttop5.min--> to 37.8%<!--c:passive.lefttop5.max--> of decisions my price left the top five levels before the order had filled under all three rules, and from then on it counts as unfilled under any rule that had not yet filled. The results file does not split this by rule, so I cannot yet say how much of each rule's unfilled share it explains. It is a large part of why fill probability sits where it does.
- **Why flat delay is not a surprise.** An order that arrives later simply joins the queue as it stands then. In this design delay changes the queue it joins only through how the book moved in between. It cannot lose a place in line it never held. A real trader who would have joined earlier loses queue priority to others, and this replay has no other traders reacting to me. So the flat result is partly built in, not proof that delay does not matter for passive orders. With random decision times the arrival is just another random moment, so a near zero change in fill probability is what the design should give whatever the market does. The fixed price arm in the Deviations section is meant to fix this.
- **Limits and gaps.**
  - *Dev day only.* H2 is judged on two test days. This is a preview.
  - *Missing:* signal driven decisions. Random decisions carry no information, so adverse selection from a stale signal is absent.
  - *Fell short:* top of book and five levels only. Cancels are inferred from size drops, so a cancel and an add of equal size in one update cancel out, and a trade stamped in a different millisecond from its book update can be counted as a cancel or an add. I have not measured how often.
  - *Fell short:* the random rule places one lump per update. Real cancels are many small orders.
  - *Not checked:* the horizon is measured from arrival, not from the decision. Markouts use the fill price as reference. These choices are logged under Deviations.
  - *Resources that would help:* order level data (Tardis incremental book) to see real queue positions, and live decision timestamps.

## 2026-11-18 (Wed, afternoon) · Yueshen read in full
- **What I read.** The open access copy from the SMU repository, which is the Articles in Advance version (pages 1 to 20, online 17 September 2025). The issue citation is Management Science 72(6):4760-4779, 2026. I read the whole text and looked at all 22<!--nc--> PDF pages as laid out, because the equations and figures don't copy cleanly out of the PDF. The proofs I read for structure, not line by line. The online appendix is not in this copy and I have not read it.
- **What it is.** A one shot theory model of a one tick book. Makers submit at about the same time, latency decides only their order in the queue, and one investor then sends a market order. The only data is a descriptive TAQ figure. There is no price path after a fill, no markout horizon and no delay in milliseconds.
- **What holds from my earlier notes.** End of queue orders lose money in equilibrium (Lemma 1, Proposition 1), and units deeper in the queue are, conditional on filling, more likely to meet a more informed market order. Clustered submissions followed by quick cancellations, which in the model fall on the tail of new queues, is the paper's first prediction. The size ratio claim I removed this morning is in the paper after all. Proposition 4(i) says the cancel to add size ratio is below one when the sector mixes fast and slow makers, because the fast orders are larger and sit near the front, so cancellations come mostly from the smaller slow orders at the back. With identical makers the ratio goes to one. Proposition 4(ii) says it rises with the share of fast makers, while one sentence in the introduction reads the other way, so I cite Proposition 4 for the direction.
- **What does not hold.** The paper never says that an order sent later has a worse markout over a fixed horizon. That step, which is H2c and my 11-15 line about measuring what a late order earns, is my own inference from the end of queue result. In the paper being late means sitting behind competitors who posted in the same burst. In my replay a later order joins whatever queue stands then, with nobody reacting to it. So the dev day matched markouts, which run against H2c, do not contradict the paper. They test a mechanism it does not model. Logged under Deviations, with the test itself unchanged.

## 2026-11-19 (Thu, late night) · jump moment split, written down before running it
Exploratory, added 2026-11-19, not a registered test. This is the split I wrote about after reading Budish, Cramton and Shim, and it was the one thing left over from this afternoon. Everything below was fixed and committed with the code (`src/s2f/jumps.py`, tests in `tests/test_jumps.py`) before I ran any of it on the dev day. What I had seen of the dev day before writing it is what is already in these notes. Spread at one tick 95.5%<!--c:audit.onetick--> of the time, the feed audit, the RQ3 volatility cut and the dev day taker numbers. I haven't counted mid moves at the ms scale or looked at anything after one.

**What a jump is.** A ticker stamp where the mid has moved by at least K bps from the mid W ms earlier. In their model a quote gets picked off when the jump is bigger than half the spread plus what it costs to take it. Half a one tick spread is under 0.01 bps on BTCUSDT, so that part never binds. The cost does. The lowest USDT taker fee on the Binance USD-M schedule as I read it today is 0.017%<!--cite-->, 1.7<!--cite--> bps, so a smaller move can't pay even the cheapest taker who hits the old quote. That gives three specs, all run and all reported.
- *A, the main line.* K = 2 bps (1.7<!--cite--> rounded up), W = 10 ms (their 2011 median arbitrage duration of 7<!--cite--> ms, rounded up).
- *B.* K = 5 bps, W = 10 ms. The regular taker fee, 0.05%<!--cite-->.
- *C.* K = 2 bps, W = 100 ms. Their example batch interval, for slower moves.

**Decision window.** A decision counts as after a jump if a jump stamp falls in the 5 ms up to and including it. For one move to count once in the jump decisions and the stale count, jumps are thinned to the first stamp and nothing within 1 s after it, and the usual gap and end of day cuts apply.

**What gets measured.** The H3 cost, top of book ΔS(100 ms) with the tie after, 5 minute block bootstrap.
- Random decisions after a jump minus the rest, and the same for signal decisions. A random decision comes every 10 s on average, so I expect very few of them to land within 5 ms of a move. That is why there is a third line.
- Jump decisions. One per thinned jump, at a time drawn uniformly in the 5 ms after it (seed 20261115 + 101<!--nc-->). The side goes with the jump, a buy after an up move, which is what a sniper does. The same decisions with the side flipped are reported too. Each is set against the random decisions outside the window.
- Stale top of book. At each thinned jump, the touch a taker going with the jump would hit, and whether that level is gone for that taker 1, 2 and 5 ms later. A second count also treats it as gone when the price is still there but less than 0.01 BTC shows. The other side of the book is done the same way, and the random decisions with their own side give the baseline.

**Too small to read.** A group with under 30 decisions or spread over under 10 five minute blocks gets its numbers reported and no reading.

**Frozen for the test days.** Specs A, B and C, the 5 ms window, the 1, 2 and 5 ms horizons, the 1 s thinning and the seed. On each test day it runs once, after the registered tests, with the signal threshold frozen at 0.9863<!--c:signal.threshold-->. It changes nothing about H1 to H5, their tests or their verdicts.

**The two papers I read tonight.** Hasbrouck and Saar touch this only through scale. Their fastest traders react to a better quote in about 2<!--cite--> to 3<!--cite--> ms, which sits inside the 5 ms window and the 1<!--cite--> to 5 ms horizons I had in mind already, so it supports the choice and doesn't change it. Cartea and Sánchez-Betancourt don't define jumps or stale liquidity. The one thing I take from them is a warning. They chose their volatility and fill windows because those made the link they were studying strongest (their footnote 4<!--cite-->), which is exactly what I'm not doing here.

**Limits and gaps.**
- *Fee date.* The fee schedule is the one shown today. It may have been different on 2026-09-01, and fast firms may pay less than the top VIP tier through rebates I can't see.
- *One venue.* Their jumps are in a related market. Mine are moves in the same book, which is the race already over, so this is a loose stand in for their idea.
